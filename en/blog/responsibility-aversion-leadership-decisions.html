Responsibility Aversion: Why the Hardest Leadership Decision Is Not the Riskiest One

Esther Michele Kazir · Nov 17, 2026 · לקריאה בעברית

A leader can be comfortable taking a risk for themselves.

And still hesitate when the outcome will land on someone else.

From the outside, that hesitation looks like indecision. Sometimes it is something else: the weight of responsibility becoming visible.

What the research suggests

A 2018 study in Science by Edelson and colleagues looked at decisions made on behalf of a group. Many participants wanted more certainty before choosing for others than before choosing for themselves. The researchers called this responsibility aversion, and people lower in it were more likely to hold leadership roles.

It is one line of research, mostly in controlled settings. It does not predict how any specific leader will behave. But it gives a precise name to something most leaders recognize.

What this means in practice

When a decision stalls, the usual diagnosis is missing information. Sometimes that is true.

Sometimes the information is enough, and what is missing is the willingness to carry the consequence. More data will not solve that one.

A question for this week

Pick one decision that has been waiting longer than it should, and ask:

Do I genuinely need more information?
Or what responsibility am I reluctant to hold?

Naming the difference does not make the decision easy. It makes it more honest.

Source: Edelson, M. G., Polania, R., Ruff, C. C., Fehr, E., & Hare, T. A. (2018). Computational and neurobiological foundations of leadership decisions. Science, 361(6401). doi.org/10.1126/science.aat0036
